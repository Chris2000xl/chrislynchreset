Reason 01
Inflammation
Chronic inflammation quietly stops your body releasing fat — and the trigger is almost certainly a food you would never suspect.
The mechanism

"Inflammation — not calories — is the reason most commonly missed in women who are doing everything right."

A woman I worked with

One of the women I coach had been bloated every single afternoon for three years. She had never once connected it to what she ate at lunch. When she removed the one food her immune system had been quietly reacting to, the bloating was gone within four days. She said it was the first time she had felt comfortable in her body after a meal in years.

What this means for you

Stubborn weight, bloating, energy crashes after eating, skin changes — these are not separate problems. They are the same mechanism expressing itself in different ways. A quiet immune response your body has been running without you knowing.

Do you experience bloating after certain meals — not every meal, but specific ones? Does your energy drop noticeably in the hour or two after eating? Have you noticed skin changes — puffiness, breakouts or dullness — that seem to follow no obvious pattern? Do you experience nasal congestion, stuffiness or mucus that has no obvious cause?

Reason 02
Cortisol
Every time you eat less to try harder, your body activates the exact stress response that makes fat loss more difficult — not less.
The mechanism

"Fasting and long gaps without food raise cortisol — and raised cortisol tells your body to store fat, not burn it. You are fighting your own stress response every time you eat less to try to push through."

A woman I worked with

A client came to me having tried intermittent fasting for six months. Her cravings were getting worse, not better, and belly fat was increasing despite the effort. Her cortisol was rising every time she extended the fasting window. The approach that was supposed to help was the thing making it harder.

What this means for you

Belly fat, cravings, poor sleep, that wired-but-exhausted feeling — cortisol rhythm disruption is the mechanism connecting all of them. Not a lack of discipline. Not getting older.

Do you currently find that going several hours without eating makes cravings feel urgent and hard to resist? Do you feel tired during the day but find it hard to switch off at night? Does your energy feel most unstable in the mid-afternoon or after a light meal? Do you experience headaches that seem connected to meal timing or stress rather than any obvious cause?

Reason 03
Oestrogen Decline
After 35, your hormonal environment changed the rules — and nobody told you, so you kept following the old ones.
The mechanism

"Oestrogen decline changes where your body stores fat — and makes it progressively harder to release that fat, even when you are eating less."

A woman I worked with

A 38-year-old came to me frustrated that her weight had shifted to her abdomen over two years despite no change in what she ate. Foods she had always tolerated were now causing bloating. She had put it down to getting older. It was not age — it was the gut lining change that follows estrogen decline. Once we identified her specific triggers, the abdominal bloating reduced significantly within three weeks.

What this means for you

Weight shifting to the abdomen, foods causing new reactions, brain fog, mood changes — these are the downstream effects of a hormonal shift that changed the rules your body plays by. Nobody updated the instruction manual.

Since your mid-30s, has weight moved to your abdomen even when your overall weight has stayed similar?

Separately — have foods that your body handled fine before started causing bloating, digestive discomfort, brain fog or mood changes? Not dramatically. Just differently than they used to.

Reason 04
Metabolic Adaptation
Every diet you have ever followed taught your body to need less — and it learned that lesson perfectly.
The mechanism

"Your body adapted to hold on every time you ate less. What changes it is not eating even less — it is removing the specific food your body has been quietly reacting to."

A woman I worked with

A woman came to me having tried every major diet over ten years. The first one worked well. Each subsequent attempt produced less. By the time she came to me, the same calorie deficit that had worked at 34 was producing almost nothing at 44. Her metabolism had adapted across a decade of restriction. What changed things was not eating less — it was removing what her immune system had been fighting the entire time.

What this means for you

Diets stopping working progressively, eating less but gaining nothing, low energy, hunger that feels harder to satisfy — when your metabolism has adapted across years of restriction, the body runs in a low-level protective state. You feel it as persistent flatness. Low energy that does not resolve. Hunger that feels urgent even when you have eaten.

Do you find your energy feels flat most of the day regardless of how much you sleep? Does hunger feel more urgent or harder to satisfy than it used to? Do you feel less motivated to move your body than you once did — not laziness, just a heaviness?

The thread that connects all four

These mechanisms do not work in isolation.
They feed each other.

Inflammation raises cortisol. Cortisol disrupts oestrogen. Oestrogen decline changes gut permeability, which drives more inflammation. Metabolic adaptation locks the whole pattern in place. This is why addressing one thing at a time has never been enough — and why understanding your specific pattern is the only thing that actually changes anything.
