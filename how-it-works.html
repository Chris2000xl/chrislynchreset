The thread that connects all four

So what is the one thing every plan has consistently missed?

All four mechanisms share a single entry point. Not a new exercise plan. Not eating even less. The specific foods your immune system has quietly learned to react to — and the two most common ones in women 35+ are dairy and gluten. Not in everyone, and not forever — but consistently enough that it is always the first place worth looking.

Here is why these two specifically: as oestrogen declines, the gut lining becomes more permeable. Proteins in dairy and gluten — casein and gliadin — pass through more easily than they used to, and your immune system registers them as a threat. That quiet immune response is exactly the inflammation from Reason 01, which keeps cortisol elevated from Reason 02 — which tells your body to hold fat regardless of how little you eat. One mechanism feeding the next, in a loop you were never shown.

This is not about these foods being bad. It is about whether your immune system has learned to fight them.

Keeping the experiment clean — where the trigger hides

Dairy also hides in non-dairy creamers, milk chocolate and some breads — scan labels for casein, caseinate, whey, lactose.

This is not forever — just a two-day experiment. Leave it out completely while you test: even a small amount keeps the response switched on.

There is nothing to get perfect here. You are simply giving your body two quiet days without the thing it may have been reacting to — and letting it show you the answer.

Path B · Reasons 02 or 04 · Cortisol & Metabolic Adaptation

Your experiment is about steadying blood sugar — not removing food.

When cortisol and metabolic adaptation are your primary pattern, the fastest felt result comes from stabilising your blood sugar across 48 hours. Your body has been running on restriction signals. This interrupts them. Nothing is removed here — this is simply about how you build each plate.

Protein · Fibre · Fat — at every meal · 48 hours

Protein + fibre + healthy fat on every plate — the combination that keeps blood sugar steady, rather than a carb on its own.

Protein: eggs · chicken · fish · prawns · Greek yogurt · beans · lentils · tofu
Fibre: veg · salad · berries · beans · lentils · oats · chia & flax seeds
Healthy fat: avocado · olive oil · nuts · seeds · olives · nut butter
Blood Sugar Stability · 48 hours
Each plate Protein + fibre + healthy fat on every plate — the combination that keeps blood sugar steady, rather than a carb on its own.
Start with breakfast Anchor the day: eggs & veg, Greek yogurt with berries, or salmon & avocado — rather than toast or cereal on its own.
Snacks Nuts, boiled eggs, or hummus with veg — something with protein or fibre, rather than a carb on its own.
Meal gaps Try not to leave long gaps — aim to eat something within about 4 hours.
Morning coffee Have something to eat before your first coffee of the day.

You are not eating less. You are building each plate so blood sugar stops spiking and crashing — and the cravings and afternoon slump ease with it.

You do not need to count anything or get it perfect. Build each plate around all three — protein, fibre and healthy fat — start with breakfast, and let the next two days show you how different steady can feel.
