One Practical Step

Now that you know why — here's where it hides.

Your Signal Decoder told you which mechanism was in play. This is the practical next step: the everyday places dairy, gluten, and sugar hide without you realising — and simple swaps for each.

WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

One food shows you part of the picture — not the whole one.

What you just tested was real. If dairy or gluten shifted something for you, that shift is genuine evidence, not a coincidence.

But here's what's also true: inflammation, cortisol, oestrogen decline, and metabolic adaptation don't work in isolation — they feed into each other. Removing one food shows you one thread. It doesn't show you the whole pattern, because the pattern was never made of just one thread.

That's not a flaw in your experiment. It's simply what testing one variable at a time can, and can't, tell you.

The swaps below still matter — dairy and gluten remain the two most common starting points for a reason. But if you try them and only feel a partial shift, that's not you doing something wrong. It's a sign there's more than one thread to pull.

BEFORE YOU START

You don't need all three of these. If inflammation or oestrogen came up as your signal, dairy and gluten are worth your attention. If cortisol or metabolic adaptation came up, sugar is the one to look at first. Either way — this isn't about cutting everything out forever. It's about knowing where to look.

One more thing worth knowing: in your first experiment, you were only ever routed to test dairy or gluten — never both — based on whether you already avoid dairy day to day, not on which one might actually matter more for you.

So if inflammation or oestrogen came up as your signal, the food you didn't test the first time is probably the most useful next step now — testing it either rules it in or rules it out, rather than leaving it an open question. If neither felt like a clean test because you already eat very little of one of them, that's useful too: it likely means that one was never going to show you much, so the other deserves your attention first.

01 · Dairy

Where it hides.

+
CHECK THE LABEL FOR
  • Non-dairy creamers — many still contain sodium caseinate
  • Protein powders and bars — whey and casein are common bases
  • Deli meats and sausages — milk proteins are used as a binder
  • "May contain milk" dark chocolate and baked goods
  • Cream-based soups, white sauces, and salad dressings
  • Some breads — milk or whey powder added for softness
EASY SWAPS
Ice cream→Frozen banana "nice cream"
Cream in soups/sauces→Coconut milk or cashew cream
Creamy salad dressing→Olive oil, lemon & herbs
Whey protein shake→Pea or hemp protein
Milk chocolate→70%+ dark chocolate
02 · Gluten

Where it hides.

+
CHECK THE LABEL FOR
  • Soy sauce — most brands contain wheat
  • Gravies and stock cubes — wheat flour is a common thickener
  • Deli meats and sausages — wheat used as a filler
  • Salad dressings and sauces
  • Oats — cross-contamination unless labelled gluten-free
  • Beer, and some spice blends (anti-caking agents)
EASY SWAPS
Soy sauce→Tamari or coconut aminos
Stock cube / gravy→Gluten-free stock, cornflour to thicken
Beer→Wine, cider, or GF beer
Plain flour for cooking→Rice or buckwheat flour
Couscous→Quinoa or rice
Breaded/battered food→GF breadcrumbs or almond flour coating
03 · Sugar

Where it hides.

+
CHECK THE LABEL FOR
  • Pasta sauces and ketchup — often surprisingly high in sugar
  • "Low-fat" flavoured yogurts — sugar often replaces the fat
  • Granola and cereal bars marketed as healthy
  • Some breads — added sugar for texture and shelf life
  • Salad dressings
  • Flavoured coffee and tea drinks, and most fruit juice
EASY SWAPS
Flavoured yogurt→Plain yogurt + berries
Granola bar→Nuts and a piece of fruit
Fizzy drink→Sparkling water + citrus
Fruit juice→Whole fruit
Needing something sweet→Cinnamon or vanilla added to food

You don't need to get this perfect.

Pick one category — dairy, gluten, or sugar, not all three at once — and try it for the next 3 to 4 days. Changing everything together makes it impossible to know what's actually helping. That's the whole task. Notice what happens, the same way you did with your first experiment.
